Behind the numbers

Method & context

Download data ↓
Data

1,336 team-season rows covering seven leagues from 2015/16 to 2024/25. Download the team tables and season gaps. Points, match counts and season-table ranks have been reconciled with RSSSF historical tables, including recorded deductions and exceptional-season rankings. The original supplied data provider remains unknown. See the sources and correction policy and correction log. This is a cross-check against a historical compilation, not a complete audit of official match records.

Averaging

PPG is recorded points divided by matches played, rounded to four decimals in the source tables. Each season has equal weight in a league’s average gaps and dispersion. First-to-last gaps use each season’s actual last team. The rank chart averages each rank separately, so Ligue 1 ranks 19–20 include only its 20-team seasons; its 2023/24 and 2024/25 tables have 18 teams.

Relative parity score

For each league, add the mean first-to-last PPG gap, the mean 1st–2nd gap and the mean within-season sample standard deviation of team PPG. Call this total D. This is equivalent to adding the three adjoining band gaps, the 1st–2nd gap and dispersion. The score is 100 × (Dmax − D) / (Dmax − Dmin), using all seven leagues over the selected seasons. If all totals are equal, every score is 50. Hiding leagues leaves scores unchanged. Changing seasons resets the scale, so scores cannot measure improvement across different season windows.

Interpretation

100 means the most even of these seven leagues under this formula; 0 means the least. Neither is an absolute measure of parity. The 1st–2nd gap overlaps the 1st–4th gap, giving extra weight to the title race. To describe where a table separates fastest, we divide each season’s band gaps by their rank steps (3, 6, and the team count minus 10), then average. The gap charts themselves show total gaps.

Limits

PPG adjusts for match counts, but does not remove differences in league size, schedules, incomplete seasons or points deductions. The table follows published season-table positions and excludes playoff matches. Ligue 1 and Eredivisie stopped early in 2019/20. The sensitivity table shows how ranks change when each selected season is left out; it is not a confidence interval. These measures describe within-season table spread, not team strength across leagues or how often different clubs win titles. See the reproduction instructions.
