Method

Method and caveats

Data: 1,336 team-season rows covering seven leagues from 2015/16 to 2024/25. Download the team tables and season gaps. The repository contains processed tables, but does not identify their original provider or collection method. Their internal consistency is checked; the original records have not been independently verified.

Averaging: PPG is recorded points divided by matches played, rounded to four decimals in the source tables. Each season has equal weight in a league’s average gaps and dispersion. First-to-last gaps use each season’s actual last team. The rank chart averages each rank separately, so Ligue 1 ranks 19–20 include only its 20-team seasons; its 2023/24 and 2024/25 tables have 18 teams.

Relative parity score: For each league, add the mean 1st–2nd, 1st–4th, 4th–10th and 10th–last PPG gaps, plus the mean within-season sample standard deviation of team PPG. Call this total D. The score is 100 × (Dmax − D) / (Dmax − Dmin), using all seven leagues over the selected seasons. If all totals are equal, every score is 50. Hiding leagues leaves scores unchanged. Changing seasons resets the scale, so scores cannot measure improvement across different season windows.

Interpretation: 100 means the most even of these seven leagues under this formula; 0 means the least. Neither is an absolute measure of parity. The 1st–2nd gap overlaps the 1st–4th gap, giving extra weight to the title race. To describe where a table separates fastest, we divide each season’s band gaps by their rank steps (3, 6, and the team count minus 10), then average. The gap charts themselves show total gaps.

Limits: PPG adjusts for match counts, but does not remove differences in league size, schedules, incomplete seasons or points deductions. Tables follow the supplied ranks. These measures describe within-season table spread, not team strength across leagues or how often different clubs win titles. See the reproduction instructions.
